# Proposal playbook — distilled from real SpotsNow proposals

Built from 11 real proposals: Xero Shoes, Kane Footwear, HealthyCell, Nextiva, SerpApi, Walmart
Marketplace, Rella (general); MASA Chips, Kalshi (remnant); Rebel + the Rebel/Quince analysis
(displacement). **Read this before every build** — it is how we actually price, structure, and voice
proposals. Mirror these patterns; only deviate when the brand or the conversation demands it.

Branding note: older docs say "Station" / "Station Fee"; 2026 docs say "SpotsNow" / "SpotsNow Fee".
Default to **SpotsNow**.

---

## 1. General-proposal skeleton (exact section order)

1. **Intro blurb** — one italic sentence (near-verbatim boilerplate, see §4).
2. **## Campaign Objectives** — Primary Goals / Secondary Goals.
3. **## Campaign Strategy** — `### Show Mix` + `### Pixel Tracking`.
4. **## Timeline** — 8 or 12 weeks, 2-column table (Program | Ad type).
5. **## Ad Creative** — Format / Style / Focus.
6. **## Proposed Budget Options** — Option A/B/C tables (1–3 options).
7. **## Performance & Reporting** — Pixel Tracking + Reporting.
8. **## Next Steps** — 1) Campaign Approval 2) Finalize Ad Creative 3) Pixel Setup & Launch, then the
   "Let's lock this in…" close.
9. **## Recommended Shows** — per-show cards: logo image + audience/impressions, demographic-match %,
   "Why we chose it", genre.
10. **Collapsible "How Are Podcast Ad Slots Priced?"** — foot of newer decks (optional).
11. **Appendices (sometimes)** — Walmart-style `Appendix A: Show Assessment` (with a "brands already
    advertising" proof column) + `Appendix B: Suggested Shows Not Included` (exclusion reasons);
    Rebel-style `More On The Strategy` / `Show Selection Methodology` essays.

**2026 additions:** a header metadata line (Point of Contact / Flight / Format / Market); a
`Premium Add-Ons` or `Precision & Reach Add-Ons` table; show names hyperlinked to live
`spotsnow.io/show/…` pages.

## 2. Pricing (anchor to these real numbers)

**Per-show monthly rates actually seen:**
- Small/niche floor: **$250/mo** (Station era) → **$300/mo** (SpotsNow era). e.g. Business Brain $250, Practical AI $300.
- Mid shows: **$400–$2,200/mo** (bulk of footwear/health rosters).
- Large/celebrity: **$4,000–$18,456/mo** (Human Upgrade $4,000; Not Gonna Lie w/ Kylie Kelce $18,000; School of Greatness $18,456).
- Premium marquee flat buys: Lenny's ~$6–20K/mo, **20VC $22K**.

**Tiers (A/B/C) = same show list, scaled.** Cleanest proof is Kane: **A = full rate, B ≈ 0.72× per
show, C ≈ 0.48× per show** (Ali on the Run $4,250 → $3,060 → $2,040). Lower tiers also drop the
biggest single-show buys. Totals land on clean numbers: **A $25,000 / B $18,000 / C $12,000**.
- A = "Bigger / Premium Reach Test" · B = "Targeted / Focused Test" · C = "Smaller / Tight
  Diversified Test."
- Recurring tier line: "uncover strong opportunities to grow ROAS quickly post test… capitalize on
  advantages that potential competitors are finding in the channel."

> Note: the older `proposal-generator` skill said "never scale a kept show's rate, only drop shows."
> The real decks do **both** — they drop the biggest buys *and* apply a per-show multiplier across
> tiers (Kane). Prefer dropping shows for small cuts; use the ~0.72× / ~0.48× multiplier when the
> client wants the same lineup at a lower commitment.

**Fee line** (separate line item just above "Monthly Total"):
- Newer: **"SpotsNow Fee (12%)"** explicit, or a **flat minimum** ("SpotsNow Fee (minimum) $1,400").
- Older: **"Station Fee"** as a flat figure ≈ 12–13% of media.
- Pixel/attribution setup is often shown as a struck-through freebie: "~~$2,500~~ **Included Free**".

**Typical total budget bands by category:** micro-SaaS/creator ~$5.6K (Rella); dev-tool $5.4–7.9K/mo
(SerpApi); footwear $12–28.6K (Kane/Xero); B2B comms $30–50K (Nextiva); parenting/DR $40K (Rebel);
enterprise marketplace $28–82K (Walmart); supplement/celebrity $135K (HealthyCell).

**Rounding:** per-show to nearest $5/$10/$25/$50; monthly totals engineered to clean figures ($12K,
$18K, $25K, $40K, $50K). CPM column shown only in celebrity/health decks (footwear ~$25–40, health
$27–62, Walmart flags $45 on premium). Remnant blended CPM runs **$17–22**.

## 4. Framing & house voice (use these near-verbatim)

- **Intro:** "This campaign is designed to run a strong initial test leveraging podcasts to unlock
  reach to untapped audiences, create strong long term ROAS and build a new and highly effective
  channel for [Brand] to scale sales, grow awareness and capitalize on their growth."
- **Primary goal:** "Aim to hit breakeven by Month 2, and scale toward 2.5–5x ROAS over time."
  (Swap a CAC target when the brand thinks in CAC, e.g. HealthyCell "a target CAC of $80 or less.")
- **Secondary goal:** "Implement audio pixel tracking to unlock full attribution (7x more visibility
  vs. untracked)."
- **Strategy staples:** "Blend of large audience + small but highly qualified shows"; "find the 3 to
  5 shows that become [Brand]'s repeatable, high-ROI channel."
- **Ad creative:** "30–60 second embedded host-read mid-rolls… Conversational and personal — less
  scripted, product endorsement."
- **Close:** "Let's lock this in — with this structure, we're confident we can hit breakeven fast and
  scale toward long-term ROI."
- **Pricing collapsible:** "Podcast ad slot pricing depends on your objectives and the target
  audience. We generally recommend a minimum of 3–4 ad slots per month… For smaller audiences,
  pricing starts at around $250 per episode and scales upward based on reach and niche."

House voice: plain, direct, confident. American spelling, no em dashes. Let proof brands and numbers
carry the argument.

## 5. Category-specific differences

- **Dev-tool (SerpApi):** smallest budgets ($5–8K/mo), tightly endemic small shows at real rate
  ($300–805), all-in transparent pricing (fee + free pixel baked in), 2-option (Core/Scale)
  structure, plus a founder/VC "premium add-on" tier to reach spend-approvers. KPI = free API-key
  sign-ups. Tone: technical, ICP-precise (demographic-match 89–97%).
- **B2B comms (Nextiva):** mid budgets ($30–50K); business-core + professional verticals
  (medicine/legal/accounting/restaurants); broad reach (1M+ downloads); two options.
- **Supplement/DTC celebrity (HealthyCell):** largest single-tier ($135K); celebrity/female + true
  crime; CPM column shown; CAC target instead of ROAS.
- **Enterprise/marketplace (Walmart):** largest and most rigorous — named POC + flight header, three
  tiers up to ~$82K/mo, explicit "SpotsNow Fee (12%)", two appendices. Differentiator: **third-party
  validation as proof** (name the sell-to-seller brand already on each show — Shopify 180 host-reads,
  Klaviyo, Helium 10) + an **exclusion appendix** with reasons ("Not in the SpotsNow catalog," "runs
  ads on only ~15% of episodes"). Flags US% weakness on international shows.
- **Footwear (Xero/Kane):** the base template — 8-week timeline, A/B(/C) tiers, no appendices,
  demographic-match % in cards.

Match the budget scale and rigor to the brand's size (Apollo revenue, see apollo-sizing.md): a
Walmart gets appendices and proof columns; a SerpApi gets tight endemic shows and transparent all-in
pricing.

## 6. Remnant format (see remnant-mode.md for the full template)

Confirmed from MASA + Kalshi. Title: "SpotsNow × [Brand] · Get the Host-Read Remnant Advantage."
Opens with the bold value-prop blockquote ("Host-read advertising is expensive, but it converts…
on remnant, at up to 40% off. We find the openings, exclude your competitors, and hand you a vetted
list…"). Grading columns: `Show | Type | Audience · F% · US% · core age | Remnant CPM (was) | Core
buyers (>7 repeat buys) | Proof brand | Key consideration`. Blended CPM audience-weighted, **$17–22**
typical; price = live discounted CPM with rate card struck in parentheses; one host-read per show =
one rotation. Always end §02 with a **"✅ First bundle we'd run"** callout naming the endemic core +
one reach show and the ~$25–30K first-flight size.

## 7. Competitor displacement (Rebel / Quince) — see competitor-displacement.md

Logic: pick one **sophisticated high-spend competitor** as the proxy signal, mine where they
repeat-buy, build the plan on that. Data: SpotsNow's "brand top podcasts by frequency (all-time)" and
"by spend (12 months)" — per show show **Ads** (all-time placements), **Audience**, **Impressions
delivered** (ads × audience), **Spend (12mo)**. Argument: "Quince spends ~$6M/month and is the most
sophisticated buyer in female-skewing lifestyle/parenting, so where they keep coming back is a strong
proxy for where [Brand] can expect to convert." Rank the competitor's top-50 by ICP fit, tag each
"already in [Brand]" vs "should be added," then bucket every show into three signal types: **direct
ICP**, **competitor top performers (show the ad count per row)**, and **competitor-pattern
lookalikes**. Carry into the plan: a single all-in monthly cap, a **last-minute spot reserve** line
to grab premium competitor placements at 30%+ off, deliberate diversification (no show >20% of
spend), and a 70–80% core / 20% exploratory thesis. Include honest data caveats (null audiences, bad
catalog rows excluded from impression math).

> Present-day nuance: when the competitor now **blocks** the category on shows it actively runs
> (the reason Rebel pivoted), lead with the competitor's **churned** shows via
> `get_brand_sponsored_shows(mode:"recently_stopped")` — proven inventory that is now open. See
> competitor-displacement.md.

## 8. Iteration signals

These are working docs, revised over time (fetch timestamps span months), not one-shot sends. Struck-
through rows show live editing (HealthyCell pulled Kristin Cavallari at $62.8 CPM). The clearest
sophistication gradient: Station→SpotsNow rebrand; flat "Station Fee"→"SpotsNow Fee (12%)"/min $1,400;
8-week→12-week timelines; 2026 additions of demographic-match %, live spotsnow.io links, proof-brand
appendices (Walmart) and strategy essays (Rebel). Newer = more proof, more rigor. Build to the newest
standard.
